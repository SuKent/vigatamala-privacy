# Vigatamala Privacy Policy (Android)

[繁體中文](/android.zh-Hant) · [简体中文](/android.zh-Hans) · [English](/android.en) · [日本語](/android.ja) · [한국어](/android.ko)

**Last updated: 26 November 2026**
**Applies to: Vigatamala for Android**

---

## In short

**Vigatamala does not send your browsing data to us.**

The app uses our account and subscription server (which records the time and type of each connection for troubleshooting, abuse prevention and usage statistics; see section 3), and the Weather, Drive Safe, Transit Companion and Calendar widgets use our weather and traffic data service (section 3, item (11)).
The app contains no analytics, crash-reporting or advertising packages; the only third-party components are Google Play's billing and review components (for buying a subscription and asking you for a rating), Google ML Kit (text, QR code and barcode recognition in camera search and Receipt lottery; it starts only when you open camera search or receipt scanning and sends usage statistics to Google; see section 3, item (7)), Google Play Integrity (used when the device account is registered, and when Weather, Drive Safe, Transit Companion and Calendar read our data service, to prove that this is the genuine app; see section 3, items (2) and (11)) and the Google Maps component (the map on a Transit Companion route page; it starts only when you open the map; see section 3, item (24)).
Which sites you visit, what you watch and what you search for stay on the device you use.

## Deleting your data

**Data kept on your phone** (browsing history, tabs, bookmarks, website data, settings…) stays only on your phone; see section 2 for how to delete each kind. **Uninstalling the app deletes all of it.**

**Data on our servers** (installation ID, device account, device manufacturer and model, subscription status, purchase records and connection records; see section 3, item (2)) is deleted when you ask us by email:

1. Open Vigatamala → "⋯" → "Settings" → "About" at the bottom → tap "Installation ID" (a single tap copies it).
2. Email **vigatamala@link2us.link** with the subject "Delete data" and paste the installation ID in the body. If you have subscribed, you can include the order number from your Google Play receipt (it starts with GPA.) instead — use it if you have already uninstalled the app and can't find your installation ID.
3. Once we have deleted it, we reply to let you know.

**What is deleted:** the device account, device links, and entitlement and subscription status.

**What is not deleted with it:**
- **The device record itself (including the installation ID and the device manufacturer and model)**: deleted automatically after 90 days without activity, except where abuse prevention requires keeping it.
- **Transaction records**: kept with your association stripped — they are the evidence used to handle refunds and purchase disputes.
- **Device-activity records** (the time and type of each connection): kept for 180 days by default.
- **Server backups**: nightly backups on the server are kept for 14 days; encrypted off-site copies are kept for 90 days, the first copy of each month for an additional 6 months, at least the 3 most recent copies of each service are always kept, and copies of a deleted service are kept for 1 year. After deletion, copies in existing backups remain until those periods end.

If you have never subscribed and can't find your installation ID, we have no way of telling which record is yours; that record is deleted automatically after 90 days without a connection.
**Data held by Google** (subscriptions, payments, and the account identifier given to Google at purchase) is in your Google Account and is handled by Google under its privacy policy; subscriptions are managed in Google Play under "Payments & subscriptions".

## 1. What we cannot receive

- **We do not receive browsing data.** The app downloads rule lists and PTT compatibility data from our domain, registers the device and syncs subscriptions, and in the Weather, Drive Safe, Transit Companion and Calendar widgets reads weather, traffic, transit and holiday data (section 3); rule and PTT compatibility-data requests carry no identifier at all, account requests contain only the identity and transaction data listed in section 3, the only identifying data in weather and traffic requests is the device key and signature used for verification (section 3, item (11)), and none of these requests contain browsing content.
- **The only third-party software packages are Google Play's billing and review components, Google ML Kit, Google Play Integrity and the Google Maps component.** No Google Analytics, Firebase Analytics, Crashlytics or Sentry, and no ad networks. The Google Play billing component, and the Google Play services components it brings in, are handled by Google under its privacy policy (section 3, item (3)); for ML Kit, see section 3, item (7) (Firebase Components and Google Data Transport, listed on the "Open Source Licenses" page, are the components ML Kit uses to send those statistics; they are not Firebase Analytics); for Play Integrity, see items (2) and (11); for the Google Maps component, see item (24).
- **No advertising ID.** The app does not declare the advertising ID permission.
- **Android WebView's usage-statistics reporting is turned off.** By default, Android's web component reports usage statistics to Google according to the phone's "Usage & diagnostics" setting; Vigatamala explicitly turns this off.
- **Websites requesting your location.** When a website asks for your location, Vigatamala first asks whether you want to give it to this site; only after you allow it does Android ask for location permission. Only "while using the app" location is requested, never background location. A website you allow can receive your location and handles it under its own privacy policy; Vigatamala does not collect or store your coordinates or send them to us. Your answer is not remembered: it applies only to that page, and you are asked again the next time you open it. You can revoke the app's location permission in Android Settings.
- **Website camera and microphone.** When a website asks to use the camera or microphone (for example, for a video call), Vigatamala first asks whether you want to give it to this site, and again your answer applies only to that page. A website you allow receives the video or audio and handles it under its own privacy policy; Vigatamala does not store it or send it to us. While a website is using them, a red icon appears in the address bar, and you can stop it at any time (this reloads that page). You can revoke the app's camera and microphone permissions in Android Settings.
- **Websites that play protected content.** Protected content such as paid video first needs an identifier that this phone issues for that website only. When a website asks for it, Vigatamala asks you first, and again your answer applies only to that page; the identifier is handled between Android and the website and is never sent to us.
- **Websites that ask you to sign in (HTTP authentication).** When a site such as a device on an internal network asks for a username and password, Vigatamala shows which site is asking; the username and password you enter are used only for this visit, are not saved on the device, and are never sent to us.
- **Local network.** When you open an address on your local network (for example a router's settings page or a home storage device), starting with Android 17 you need to allow the "Nearby devices" permission, which Android asks for. Vigatamala asks only when you open such an address. Android's prompt uses fixed wording: "find, connect to, and determine the relative position of nearby devices"; Vigatamala uses this permission only to connect to the address you opened, does not scan your local network, does not determine the position of any device, and never sends us where you connected. Once you allow it, content in the web pages you open can also connect to your local network.
- **Uploading files.** When a web page asks you to choose a file, you pick it from Android's photo picker or file picker, or take a photo or video on the spot (the phone's camera app opens only when you tap). Only the files you choose are given to that page; Vigatamala does not keep them or send them to us. A file taken on the spot is captured by the camera app and given to that page as is (any additional data the camera app writes, such as the location where it was taken, depends on your camera settings); it is kept temporarily in the app's cache and deleted the next time you open the app.
- **Camera search.** The camera button in the address bar opens the camera (Android asks for camera permission the first time); you can also pick a photo from your library instead (through Android's photo picker: Vigatamala receives only the photo you pick, cannot see the rest of your library, and does not need photo library permission). QR codes and text on screen or in the photo are recognized only on this device (using Google ML Kit; images and recognition results are not sent out; for ML Kit's own usage statistics, see section 3, item (7)); when a code contains a web address, the site name is shown first and you decide whether to open it. Tapping a piece of text lets you search it, put it in the address bar, or translate it (conversion between Simplified and Traditional Chinese is done on the device; other languages go to the Android system's offline translation, whose language data the system downloads; Vigatamala does not keep the original text or the translation). Only when you tap "Search Photo with Google" (or the other search engine named on the button) is the photo you just took or picked scaled down and sent to that search engine (section 3, item (8)); the photo carries no location or other attached data, and Vigatamala does not keep it or send it to us. The search results page is recorded in browsing history like any other web page (not in private tabs).
- **Voice search.** The first time you tap the microphone in the address bar, Android asks for microphone permission. When you speak, recognition runs only on this device: Vigatamala uses only Android's on-device speech recognition, does not send your voice over the network, and does not keep the recording; the recognized text is used only to run that one search and is then handled the same way as a search you type yourself. On Android 13 and later, if the speech-recognition data for your language hasn't been downloaded yet, tapping the microphone first explains this; after you tap "Download", the Android system (Google's speech services) downloads it — neither the downloaded content nor the connection passes through Vigatamala, and neither includes your voice; after the download, recognition still happens only on this device. If this device can't recognize your language on the device (or on Android 11 and earlier), the microphone button does not appear. You can revoke the app's microphone permission in Android Settings.
- **Start-page widgets (in builds that support this feature).** You can place widgets below "Shortcuts" on the start page. Some widgets are offered only when your region is Taiwan (judged from your phone's region setting, not from your location). A tile shows only the icon and name; a tool that is making sound gets a speaker badge (meaning only that it is playing). Nothing inside a widget is shown. Widget data is never included in system backups (section 2).
- **Receipt lottery (in builds that support this feature; offered when your region is Taiwan).** The Receipt lottery widget on the start page lets you scan the barcode or number on a receipt with the camera (Android asks for camera permission the first time) or enter the last three digits to check for a prize. Recognition happens only on this device (using Google ML Kit; images and recognition results are never sent out; for ML Kit's own usage statistics, see section 3, item (7)). Receipts you scan or enter are **never saved** and are not sent to us; they are gone when you close the screen.

If you allow "Alarms & reminders" in Android, the app schedules an exact system alarm shortly before the end to wake itself, so the five-second beeps sound even when you have left the app; if you don't, they sound only while the app is open. Picture-in-picture shrinks the whole app into a small window, and the countdown carries on. All of this happens on the phone and goes over no network (for clock sync, see section 3, item (16)).
- **Current location in Weather (in builds that support this feature).** **Weather inside the app**: only after you turn on "My Location" in the Weather widget and allow location in Android (only "While using the app" is requested) does the app read your location while you are looking at the weather (once when you open it; again if you come back after more than 10 minutes; never in the background), first rounding it to two decimal places (about 1 km). The county or city and the township are worked out on the phone from Taiwan's Ministry of the Interior boundaries; place names are never looked up with any service. To get the weather, this roughly 1 km coordinate is sent to our data service, and our server asks Apple Weather on the app's behalf (section 3, item (11)); in Taiwan with "Taiwan weather source" set to the Central Weather Administration, only the **county or city** code is sent to our data service, never the coordinates or the township. The location read here stays only in memory; turning off "My Location" in "Weather Locations" stops the reading. **The Home Screen weather widget** is separate: a newly added weather widget shows "My Location" by default (you can choose a city when you place it, or later by long-pressing the widget → "Settings"). The widget **does not read your location**: when it shows the current location, it uses the roughly 1 km coordinate that Weather in the app read last (valid for 6 hours; after that it shows that the location is temporarily unavailable, and opening Weather in the app updates it). That coordinate and the time it was read are kept in the app's own storage on this device (not included in backups; section 2), so that when you open the app, and during background updates scheduled by Android (about once an hour; they can happen even when the app isn't open), the app can get the weather for it, sending the same as above. To stop: choose a city or remove the widget; when no widget shows the current location, the app deletes the stored coordinate.

Tapping "Stop Driving Alerts" ends it. While driving, the app also reads the phone's barometer (no permission needed) only on this device to tell whether you are on an elevated road or the surface road below; the readings stay only in memory. Your driving coordinates, track and barometer readings are **not sent to us or anyone else**, and apart from a parking spot you choose to save (next bullet), nothing is stored. The comparison uses public data downloaded in full (section 3, items (11) and (12)).
- **Parking spot in the Drive Safe widget (in builds that support this feature).** If you choose to save it when you stop driving alerts, or tap "Save Current Location" in "Parking Spot", the app stores that one location, the floor, area, space number and note you enter, and one photo of the space you take on this device (not included in backups); it is **not sent to us or anyone else**. Only one is kept, and a new one replaces the old; tapping "Picked Up Car" deletes it together with the photo. The photo is re-compressed and contains no location. Only when you tap "Navigate in a Maps App" is the location handed to the maps app you choose.

When you tap "Walk Me to the Stop", the **stop's** location is handed to Google Maps (or another maps app if it isn't installed) for walking directions; your location is not sent.
- **Get-off and arrival alerts in Transit Companion (in builds that support this feature; offered when your region is Taiwan).** After you tap "Get-Off Alert" or "Arrival Alert", the app keeps reading your precise location on this device (the "While using the app" permission): a get-off alert uses it to count the stops you pass and notify you as your stop gets close; an arrival alert uses it to estimate how long it takes you to walk to the stop and tell you when to leave. Ride history (when you boarded, when you arrived, the route and plate number, from which stop to which, how many stops) is stored only on this device, is not included in backups, is kept for at most a year and can be deleted in Transit Companion.

Sound is processed only on this device and played through the speaker; it is not sent to us or anyone else, and live amplification records nothing. When the link uses Bluetooth instead, Android 12 or later asks for the "Nearby devices" permission; if you don't allow it, the phones can connect only over a shared Wi-Fi network, nothing else is affected, and you can change it later in Android Settings.

The amounts and expressions you type **stay only in memory**; they are not saved and not sent to us or anyone else, and they are gone when you close the screen. Exchange rates are public data downloaded when you open the widget (section 3, item (17)).
- **Radio (in builds that support this feature).** The Radio widget on the start page finds internet radio stations and podcasts to listen to online. The text you search for and the country (by default, your phone's region setting) are sent to the station directory or Apple's podcast directory, and audio plays directly from the station's or publisher's servers (section 3, item (18)); **none of these connections go through us**, and we don't know what you listen to. Saved stations, subscribed shows, recently played stations and podcast playback progress are stored only on this device (section 2). While playing, Android's notification, the lock screen and a connected car show the station or show name (see "Media notifications" at the end of section 3); the start-page tile shows only a speaker badge, not what you are listening to.

When someone on your list logs on and PTT sends a log-on notice, the app just shows a notice and does not record it. Profile details, account records, signatures and profile cards read in "Author Info", "Post Info" and "Settings" stay only in memory. When you copy a verification code, recovery code or key, it goes to the system clipboard (on Android 13 or later it is marked as sensitive, so keyboards and previews don't show it); it is not cleared automatically, and copying something else replaces it. Throwing water balls (instant messages), reading your friend list and adding or removing friends are also done by the app pressing PTT's keys for you over the BBS connection; water balls received and sent are recorded in the PTT message history on this device (see section 2), and the friend list stays only in memory. Chatting with someone (the site's "Talk") is also the app pressing PTT's keys for you over the BBS connection; the chat appears only on screen, the app does not store it, and when it ends you can choose to save it to your own PTT mailbox. **Adult boards are hidden by default**: turning them on in PTT "Settings" asks for your birth year and opens them only if you are 18 or older (the birth year is used only for that check and is not saved); after that, each time you open the PTT widget, you are asked once more before entering an adult board. The sensors used by the barometer, slope and level, picture-hanging aid, compass and magnetometer need no permission. Each tool's readings, calibrations, zeroing, baselines and bearing marks, and what you enter in unit conversion, unit price, split bill and tip, date calculator and counter (numbers, dates and names), stay only in memory and are cleared when you leave that page; **the only thing Toolkit saves is the ruler calibration** (section 2).

Bots are calculated on the host; the narration in Village and Wolves is read aloud with Android's speech synthesis (always on the host; on other phones only if you switch "Narration: Off" to "Narration: On" in "⋯"; it reads seat numbers only, never names); neither goes over the network. In Village and Wolves, when it is your turn to speak in the daytime, you can "Hold to Talk": while you hold, the microphone's sound is passed live through the host to the phones at the same table and played (Android asks for microphone permission the first time); **nothing is recorded or saved**, and it stops when you let go. ## 2. Data stored on your device

The browsing data in the table below stays only on your device. **The installation ID, subscription sync data, the device key for the weather and traffic data service, and the roughly 1 km location used for weather are exceptions**; their purpose and what is sent are described in section 3.

**System backup: only settings are backed up.** When Vigatamala uses Android's backup service, it backs up a single settings file: your switches and preferences such as the search engine, **plus any exceptions you have set for individual sites (including those sites' host names)**.
If backup is on for your Google Account, this settings file is stored in Google's backup service; we cannot read the backup's contents.
Browsing history, tabs, bookmarks, the reading list, website data and the installation ID are not in the backup. Start-page widget data (the rows marked "in builds that support this feature" below) is never included in backups; on a new phone you set them up again.

**Website data needs a fairly recent Android System WebView.** If WebView is too old to support separate data profiles: all normal tabs share one set of website data, and closing a tab does not erase it; private tabs can't be opened; and Crisis Prep's official maps don't clear their cookies either.

| Data | Contents | Retention | How to delete |

## 3. Outbound connections the app makes

Apart from the pages you browse yourself, the app makes only these kinds of connections:

**(1) Blocking rule downloads/updates → `privacy.link2us.link` (fallback: `easylist.to`)**
EasyList and EasyPrivacy are not bundled with the app; you download them yourself (the app asks once on first launch, and you can download them at any time in Settings → "Content Blocking"). **Downloading does not require a subscription.**
By default they come from our mirror as pre-converted, signed files (verified on device); only if the mirror is unavailable does the app fall back to fetching the raw lists from `easylist.to` and converting them on your device.
What the subscription buys is "Automatic Rule Updates" (off by default): once it is on, the app checks for updates automatically when it launches or returns to the foreground, but only if more than 8 hours have passed since the last check and the network is not metered.
**What the request carries:** only the filename to download and its signature file, plus the file-version tag the server gave at the last download (`If-None-Match`; it identifies that file, not you, and is identical for everyone holding the same list); the User-Agent is a fixed "Vigatamala". Redirects requested by the server are followed. No URL you visited, no account or device identifier, no cookies, and nothing written to the cache.
**What reaches us:** as with any web server, the connection itself carries your IP address, the time and the requested filename. That domain is static file hosting (Cloudflare); we run no code of our own on it and **we do not export or retain per-request logs anywhere**; what we can see is the provider's aggregate traffic statistics (request counts, cache hit rate and the like), which contain no individual users. Your IP address is still processed by the provider in the course of delivery and abuse prevention — unavoidable in any connection. When the app falls back to `easylist.to`, it is EasyList's infrastructure (a third party) that sees your IP, outside our hands.

**(2) Device accounts and subscription sync → `apple.link2us.link`**
When the app launches (after it has first asked Google Play which purchases there are), each time you return to the app's screen (including coming back from the camera, a file picker or Google Play), when a purchase completes, and when you tap "Restore Purchases", the app sends over HTTPS the installation ID, the app's package name (`com.sanlong.vigatamala`), the platform `android`, the app version number, and the phone's manufacturer and model (for example "Google" and "Pixel 7"; `deviceManufacturer`/`deviceModel`; reading them needs no permission, and no serial number, Android ID, advertising ID or build fingerprint is sent), to create or recover a device account; later requests carry the session token issued by the server. When creating or restoring the device account (about once a day), the app also asks Google Play Integrity to confirm it is the genuine, unmodified app installed from Google Play, and sends the token Google returns (`integrityToken`); our server passes it to Google to decode, checks only whether it passes, and does not keep the token or the verdict. Google processes the device and app information needed for that check under its privacy policy.
When there is a Plus purchase, it also sends: the package name, product ID, Google Play purchase token, purchase time, order number, and the account identifier given to Google at purchase (the installation ID of the installation that made the purchase; after you reinstall or switch phones, it is sent together with the current installation ID). The backend verifies the purchase with Google and then queries the subscription status.
The User-Agent is a fixed "Vigatamala". **No browsing history, URLs, searches, bookmarks or viewed media are sent.** Connection services still process necessary connection information such as IP addresses.
Purchase records that can't be sent stay on the device and are retried (after 1 minute, 5 minutes, 30 minutes, 2 hours and 6 hours, then at most once a day; they are sent only when you open or return to the app).
This data is used for account association and subscription service; for the manufacturer and model, the server keeps only the first pair it receives and uses it to tell Google's automated test devices apart, to rule out abnormal devices and to count how device models are distributed (administrators can see an individual account's model). The server also records the time and type of each connection (launch, return to foreground, subscription sync) in the account's device-activity records, which are used for troubleshooting, abuse prevention and aggregate usage statistics (such as daily active devices, retention and app-version distribution); administrators can view these records for an individual account. They are not provided to third parties and are not used for advertising or cross-app tracking. Uninstalling the app or clearing browsing data does not delete the records on the server; to delete or access them, see "Deleting your data" above (the scope of deletion and retention, the periods, and server backups are all described there). Account and transaction data are retained as long as needed to provide the service and handle purchase disputes.

**(3) In-app purchases → Google Play**
Google Play handles purchasing, restoring and managing subscriptions; payment details (such as credit cards) are collected by Google, and neither the app nor we can obtain them. Each time the app launches or returns to the foreground, it asks Google Play on the phone for this Google Account's subscriptions and, when needed, acknowledges a purchase. At purchase, the app gives Google the installation ID as the account identifier, and Google stores it in that transaction. The Google Play billing component handles data under Google's privacy policy.

**(4) Safe Browsing → Google**
Android's web component has Google Safe Browsing built in: it checks the URLs you visit with Google for known fraudulent or malicious sites, and blocks the ones that match. **This is Google's feature; the query goes to Google, and we can neither see nor influence it.**
We choose to keep it on (turning it off would shorten this policy by one item but leave you with one less layer of protection; the iOS version keeps Apple's fraudulent website warning for the same trade-off).

**(5) Images inside reader view → the article's own source and its image hosts**
Reader view still loads the original article's images after reflowing, applying the same blocking rules as that tab.

**(6) Site icons → the site you are viewing**
To show site icons on tab cards and bookmarks, the app fetches the icon, within the page you have open, from that site or from the icon address it declares (for example, the CDN it uses) (at most 4 times per page); no third-party icon service is queried.

**(7) Camera search usage statistics → Google (ML Kit)**
Text, QR code and barcode recognition in camera search and Receipt lottery uses Google ML Kit: the models are bundled in the app and recognition happens on the device, so **images and recognition results are never sent out**. ML Kit does, however, send usage statistics to Google for diagnostics and usage analytics: device information (manufacturer, model, OS version, available ML hardware accelerators), the app's package name and version, a per-installation identifier (which, according to Google, is not used to identify users or physical devices), performance metrics (such as latency), API configuration (such as image format and resolution), input and output sizes, feature version, event types and error codes. According to Google, this data is sent over HTTPS and is not passed to third parties. These statistics may be sent later, on a schedule set by the system, after you have finished using camera search. **There is no way to turn these statistics off**; if you have never opened camera search or receipt scanning, ML Kit does not start.

**(8) Photo search → the search engine named on the button**
Sent only when you tap "Search Photo with ○○"; what is sent is that one photo (scaled down, re-encoded, with no attached data). It is sent in the current tab, just as if you had uploaded the photo on that site yourself, and the search engine handles it under its own privacy policy; we receive nothing.

**(9) Crisis Prep → official maps and links you open**
Crisis Prep connects only when you open an official map (`adr.npa.gov.tw`, `bear.emic.gov.tw` and their map tile servers), which is the same as opening those two sites in the browser: it uses a separate web view that is not subject to content blocking, **leaves no browsing history or cookies**, and asks you first when a site requests your location (as in section 1). Saved views stay only on this phone. Links in the handbook, the official websites and the guides connect only when you open them (in a sub-page, like ordinary web pages); links to official apps are handed to Google Play. Siren sounds, lists and guides are all in the app and work offline; dialing only opens the phone's dialer, and it is you who taps to call.

**(10) Ratings → Google Play**
After the app has been installed for a full seven days and you have used it for a while, the app asks Google Play to ask you once whether you'd like to rate it — only once, ever. The window is shown by Google Play, which also decides whether to actually show it; the stars and review you give go directly to Google Play and are published on the store under Google's rules. We can't see whether you rated, and we receive nothing. The app sends no browsing data for this.

**(11) Weather, traffic, transit and calendar data → `data.link2us.link` (Weather, Drive Safe, Transit Companion and Calendar widgets; in builds that support this feature)**
This is our own service (relayed through Cloudflare), providing public data prepared in advance: forecasts, warnings and station observations (temperature, pressure, visibility) from Taiwan's Central Weather Administration; air-quality observations and forecasts, UV observations and dust forecasts from Taiwan's Ministry of Environment; speed cameras; routes and speed limits of Taiwan's national freeways; routes and speed limits of provincial highways (mainly government open data, with elevated-section markings and segmentation drawn from OpenStreetMap; the whole set is licensed under the ODbL); speed limits of ordinary roads (compiled from OpenStreetMap under the ODbL); live traffic conditions; the positions of New Taipei City ambulances and fire engines on duty; Taipei and New Taipei bus routes, stops, arrival times and vehicle positions; MRT, Taiwan Railway and High Speed Rail stations and timetables; bus route stop sequences; and official holidays in various regions. Apart from the sets that contain OpenStreetMap content, everything is government open data. It also asks Apple Weather for weather on behalf of the Android version (the way Apple Weather offers to apps can't be used on Android). The app connects only in these cases:

- **When you look at the weather (Apple Weather)**: the app sends the grid cell rounded to about 1 km (for example, the cell at "25.03°N, 121.56°E"; in a header, not in the URL), and our server asks Apple Weather for the current, hourly and ten-day forecasts and passes them back. This is not used for places in Taiwan when "Taiwan weather source" is set to the Central Weather Administration. **Our server keeps the result in memory for only 10 minutes** (asking again for the same cell within ten minutes doesn't go to Apple again); it is not written to disk, logs or backups, and no account or device identifier is sent to Apple. The app loads Apple Weather's logo from the address Apple provides. During background updates (about once an hour), the Home Screen weather widget also has the place it shows looked up this way.
- **When you look at the weather for a place in Taiwan**: the app reads the Central Weather Administration's warnings, one set for all of Taiwan, whichever "Taiwan weather source" you chose. The request contains no location, county, city or township; the phone works out which county or city you are in by itself.
- **When "Taiwan weather source" is set to "Central Weather Administration"**: the app also reads the township forecasts for your county or city. What is sent is that **county or city**'s dataset code (in a header, not in the URL); coordinates and townships are never sent. When a Home Screen weather widget shows a city in Taiwan (or the current location is in Taiwan), the app also reads that county or city's forecast for it when you open the app and during background updates scheduled by Android (which can happen even when the app isn't open), at most once every fifteen minutes per place; what is sent is the same, only the county or city code. When you look at the weather in the Weather widget, the app also reads the station observations and Ministry of Environment data, one set for all of Taiwan (each station's latest air quality and UV observations, air-quality forecasts for each air-quality zone, and dust forecast bulletins); the request contains no location, and the phone works out the nearest station and your air-quality zone by itself. The Home Screen weather widget reads only the station observations (using a nearby station's temperature as the current temperature).
- **When you use the Drive Safe widget**: the app downloads the speed cameras, freeway and provincial-highway routes and speed limits, ordinary-road speed limits and live traffic conditions, each a single set shared by all of Taiwan. The camera locations and each route and speed-limit set are checked for a new version about once a day; while driving alerts are on, traffic conditions are read about every 1 to 2 minutes. **When you are around New Taipei City**, the app also reads, about every 20 to 30 seconds, the positions of on-duty ambulances and fire engines published by the New Taipei City Fire Department (one set for the whole city, not filtered by your location). The requests contain no location; all matching happens on the phone, and whether you are around New Taipei City is also worked out by the phone itself.
- **When you use the Transit Companion widget**: the app reads the Taipei and New Taipei bus route and stop lists, the MRT / Taiwan Railway / High Speed Rail station lists, and the arrival times and timetables for the route, stop or origin–destination pair you open. What is sent is the data name and that route's, stop's or station pair's code (in a header, not in the URL); **the requests contain no location, no text you search for and not your favorites**. While the screen is open, buses are read about every 20 seconds and MRT and trains about every 10 minutes; while a get-off or arrival alert is running, that route's arrival data is read about every 20 seconds; nothing is read while the app is off screen (and not shrunk into picture-in-picture). Trip planning also reads a public list of "route stop sequences" (which stops each route passes through, in order; at most once a day, stored on the phone); where you are going from and to is worked out only on the phone and is not sent.
- **When you use the Calendar widget**: when you open Calendar, the app reads a shared holiday directory, then the holiday tables for the region you chose and the years you are looking at; if nothing has changed, they are not downloaded again. What is sent is the data name and that holiday table's code (in a header, not in the URL); **the requests contain none of your events, shift schedules or calendars**.

**Every request includes**: the identifier and signature of a key generated in the Android Keystore for this installation, used to confirm that requests come from the same installation of the genuine app; on first use, the app also asks Google Play Integrity to vouch that it was installed from Google Play and is running on an unmodified phone (Google handles the device and app information needed for that under its privacy policy; our server receives only Google's verdict and the key's public key, and does not keep the verdict). Also included are the app's package name, the data name and the version tag from the last download; the user-agent string is the fixed "Vigatamala/1.0 (Android)" (without the Android version or phone model). **No account, no installation ID from item (2) and no other device identifier is included**; this key is not linked to the account in item (2). Connections carry no cookies and write no disk cache. After you reinstall the app, the key is gone and the app uses a new one.

**What we keep on our side**:
- **Key records**: one per key, containing the app's package name, the platform (android), that key's public key and when it was registered.
  - The key identifier is a hash of that public key, which amounts to **a fixed code for each installation**.
  - The records contain **no county or city, location, IP address or time of each read**. They are deleted automatically 90 days after the last successful read, and each read extends this. Background updates of the Home Screen weather widget also count as reads: while it is still on the Home Screen, the record is extended even if you don't open the app.
  - Server backups also contain these records: nightly local backups are kept for 14 days, and encrypted off-site copies for 90 days or more (see "Deleting your data" for the rules).
- **One-time verification records**: expire within 2 minutes and are deleted once used. They contain only the key, the purpose and "weather, traffic, transit or calendar", with no county, city or location.
- **The roughly 1 km grid cell, county or city codes, Transit Companion route, stop and station codes, and Calendar holiday-table codes**: used only in memory to look up and return that data (the grid cell is also kept for 10 minutes as described above), and **never written to any storage, log or backup**.
- **Abuse-prevention counters**: grouped by a hash of the IP address and gone after 60 seconds. The key used for the hash is generated at random each time the service starts and is never saved.
- **Service logs**: requests are not logged individually.

**What isn't finished yet**:
- Connections go through Cloudflare (our network provider). While relaying, it can see the whole request (including the roughly 1 km grid cell, county or city codes, Transit Companion route and stop codes, and Calendar holiday-table codes) and your IP address, and it samples and logs IP addresses, URLs and times, keeping them for up to 30 days (the URLs contain neither the grid cell nor the county or city).
- Forecasts for different counties and cities differ in size (about 62 KB to 1.8 MB), so anyone who can see traffic statistics (including Cloudflare) could in theory infer the county or city from the size.
- The account service in item (2) also goes through the same Cloudflare account, so anyone who can see both could match requests on the two sides by IP address and time. We do not do such matching.

**When it can't connect**: if this service can't be reached, or this phone temporarily can't pass verification (for example, because the app wasn't installed from Google Play or the phone has been modified), the app does not fall back to unverified requests. Weather explains on screen that it is temporarily unavailable; the Drive Safe widget downloads directly from government websites instead (item (12)); Transit Companion explains that it is temporarily unavailable and does not switch to another source; Calendar keeps using the holiday tables already on the phone, or the tables built into the app if there are none, and says so on screen.

## 4. Private tabs

**What they do:**

- Nothing is written to browsing history or to the tab list, and they are not restored when you reopen the app — **except the ones you ask to keep**: pinned private tabs, and pinned or passcode-protected private groups (with every tab in them),
  are kept after you close the app, **sign-ins included**. The URLs, titles and groups (name, color, the passcode's hash and salt, and the number of wrong guesses) of those kept tabs are written to a file on the device that is not backed up;
  once you unpin them or remove the passcode, they are no longer kept, and that copy is deleted the next time you open the app. Only a hash of the passcode is stored; a forgotten passcode can't be recovered, and the only option is to close the whole group (no passcode needed).
- Each private tab's website data (cookies, caches, localStorage) is its own copy: it is stored on this device while in use and **emptied when you close the tab**; Android doesn't allow a folder that has been used to be deleted during the same run, so the folder itself is deleted the next time you open the app. If the system ends the app outright, it is likewise deleted the next time you open the app (except for kept private tabs). If Android System WebView is too old, private tabs can't be opened (see section 2).
- The group passcode stops "someone holding your unlocked phone from opening the group", not "someone who has the device's files": the URLs of the tabs in the group are still written in plain text in that file, protected by Android's own data encryption and screen lock.
- Titles are not sent to media notifications, the lock screen or car displays.
- The playback queue stays in memory only and is never written to the device.
- No tab thumbnails are captured, and site icons are not written to files.
- The address bar lists no suggestions until you start typing; once you type, it still matches against your local history and bookmarks (all computed on the device). To turn off that part too, Settings → "Privacy" has "No Local Suggestions in Private Tabs".
- Shield settings you adjust for private tabs apply only to this session and are forgotten once the last private tab is closed.
- **If you leave the app while private content is on screen, its card in "Recents" shows no screenshot** (Android 13 and later). Android 12 and earlier don't have this option, so that card shows the screen as it was when you left.

**Actions you take deliberately still leave traces:** adding a bookmark or saving to the reading list from a private tab writes that URL into the list (and remembers that it was saved from a private tab, so it opens in a private tab again).

## 5. What we change on web pages

- **Block** connections to ads and trackers according to the rules, and hide the empty slots left behind after blocking.
- **Intercept** pop-ups that sites open on their own, new-tab hijacking and back-button hijacking.
- **Fingerprint noise**: add subtle noise when sites read device characteristics such as the canvas, making it harder to recognize the same phone across sites.
- **Automatically decline** non-essential cookies in cookie consent windows, and **dismiss** "open in app" promotion walls.
- **Strip** common tracking parameters from URLs, and restore AMP pages to the original page; night mode gives web pages dark colors.
- **User-Agent**: ordinary web pages get the default string of Android's web component (which includes the Android version and phone model, the same as the built-in browsers of most Android apps); "Request Desktop Site" in the shield menu switches to desktop Chrome's string.
- **Global Privacy Control**: `navigator.globalPrivacyControl` on the page is true; loads we initiate ourselves (the address bar, bookmarks, links opened from outside the app) carry `Sec-GPC: 1`; this header can't be added to links clicked within a page or to subresources.
- **HTTPS upgrade**: if a site can't be reached after upgrading, the app asks whether you want to use HTTP instead; once you agree, that site is not upgraded again until you close the app.
